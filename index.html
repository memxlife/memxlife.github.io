My work spans computer systems and artificial intelligence, including machine learning systems, embodied intelligence, VLSI and electronic design automation. Before joining NUS, I was a professor at Fudan University and a faculty member at the University of Colorado Boulder, and I served as Deputy Director and Chief Architect of Intel Labs China. Research Vision

My long-term vision is to build personalized embodied intelligent systems — small, continuously learning and self-evolving artificial agents that understand and interact with the physical world. By bridging machine learning algorithms with system software and silicon, I seek to develop adaptive cyber-physical intelligence that co-evolves with individuals over time, serving as intelligent companions that augment human capability, support lifelong growth, and deepen personal understanding of the world.

Research Directions

1. Bridging Machine Learning Algorithms with System Software and Silicon

AI systems work best when the model, the software that runs it and the chip underneath are designed together. I study how to co-design models, compilers, runtimes and hardware architectures across these layers, and how AI agents can help formulate and solve such cross-layer design problems.

2. Efficient Machine Learning Systems

Training and serving large models costs a great deal of computation, memory and energy. I build training and inference systems that use these resources well, run reliably at scale, and give a clear trade-off between performance and energy.

3. Physical & Embodied Intelligence

Intelligence that lives in the physical world has to sense, act and keep learning. I explore small, adaptive agents that learn continually from real-world interaction, connect perception with action, and work alongside people over long periods of time.
