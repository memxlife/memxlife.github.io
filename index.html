Research Vision

My long-term vision is to build personalized embodied intelligent systems — small, continuously learning and self-evolving artificial agents that understand and interact with the physical world. By bridging machine learning algorithms with system software and silicon, I seek to develop adaptive cyber-physical intelligence that co-evolves with individuals over time, serving as intelligent companions that augment human capability, support lifelong growth, and deepen personal understanding of the world.

Research Directions

1. AI Infrastructure: Software to Silicon

AI infrastructure is still designed in layers: the model, the software that runs it and the chip are built on separate tracks, so gains that need them to change together are missed. I study how AI agents can design across these layers, from serving software and compilers down to the chip microarchitecture. The agent writes the design problem, solvers search it, and verification and measurement check the result.

2. Efficiency and Scalability of Large Models

Large models keep growing, and so does the cost of training and serving them. I work on making large models run faster and cheaper as they scale, across computation, memory, communication and energy, so that more capable models can be trained and served on the hardware we have.

3. Physical & Embodied Intelligence

Intelligence that lives in the physical world has to sense, act and keep learning. I explore small, adaptive agents that learn continually from real-world interaction, connect perception with action, and work alongside people over long periods of time.
